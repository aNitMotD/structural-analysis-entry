# Generalized Structure Analysis Example

## 0. Purpose

This document is **an example document intended to support understanding of generalized structural analysis**.

The purposes of this document are as follows.

- To help readers intuitively understand the general sequence of structural analysis.
- To explain, through examples, the sense of reading backward from an already completed result toward the hidden dividing lines, joining surfaces, and assembly order.
- To organize, in analogical form, what boundary, transition, and judgment point mean in structural analysis.

This document is **an example document intended to support general understanding**, and does not deal with any specific formalization method unique to a particular system or high-density observational structure.

That is, the focus of this document lies in the following.

- understanding the sequence of general structural analysis
- intuitive examples of the difference between structure and surface
- interpretation of boundary, transition, and joining through the plastic model analogy

Conversely, the following are not within the direct scope of this document.

- proprietary formalization structures
- definition systems for specific observational units
- high-density documentation methods that combine higher-order coordinates and lower-level structures

---

## 1. The Sequence of General Structural Analysis

General structural analysis may broadly be understood in the following sequence.

1. **Defining the object**  
   First determine what is to be examined.

2. **Separating the elements**  
   Divide the main components that make up the object.

3. **Identifying the relations**  
   Examine how the elements are connected to one another.

4. **Setting the boundary**  
   Determine up to where something is to be regarded as the same unit.

5. **Checking the transition**  
   Examine under what conditions a state or role changes.

6. **Extracting patterns**  
   Extract recurring forms, common structures, and frequently appearing branches.

7. **Checking exceptions**  
   Check conditions under which the structure is not maintained, or counterexamples.

8. **Summarizing the structure**  
   Organize the main elements, relations, boundaries, transitions, and exceptions.

Compressed into one line, it is as follows.

> defining the object → separating the elements → identifying the relations → setting the boundary → checking the transition → extracting patterns → checking exceptions → summarizing the structure

## 2. Why the Plastic Model Analogy Is Appropriate

When looking at a completed plastic model, most people first see the following.

- outer appearance
- color
- proportions
- surface connections

That is, they see the **outer surface** of the completed result.

However, to see structure is closer to reading one layer below that.

- where the dividing lines are
- where the joining surfaces are
- in what order it was assembled
- which parts are integrated and which parts are separated
- which joints determine the overall form

That is, structural analysis is not **the act of looking at the completed surface**, but is closer to **the act of reading backward toward the dividing lines and joining boundaries hidden behind the completed object**.

This analogy is appropriate for the following reasons.

1. It shows surface and structure as separate.
2. It makes one understand boundaries not as mere decorative lines, but as actual joining points.
3. It reveals that structural analysis is not description of results, but reverse inference.

## 3. The Plastic Model Analogy According to the Sequence of General Structural Analysis

If the sequence of general structural analysis is translated into the plastic model analogy, it can be seen as follows.

### 3.1 Defining the object = deciding which completed object to look at

First determine the completed object that becomes the target of analysis.

Examples:
- one robot plastic model
- one car plastic model
- looking only at one specific part in enlarged form

That is, first fix what is to be read as the structural unit.

### 3.2 Separating the elements = dividing what parts it is made of

Looking at the completed object, divide the following.

- head
- torso
- arms
- legs
- decorative parts
- joint parts
- outer shell and inner frame

That is, distinguish the main components that make up the whole.

### 3.3 Identifying the relations = seeing which part is connected to what

Now examine the connections among the parts.

- how the arm is attached to the torso
- how the joint interlocks with the outer shell
- which part supports another part

That is, examine the relations of connection among the elements.

### 3.4 Setting the boundary = identifying up to where one part extends

At this point, merely seeing shape and seeing structure begin to diverge.

- where one part ends
- where another part begins
- whether something looks like one unit but is actually separated
- conversely, whether something looks divided but is actually integrated

That is, examine the boundary.

### 3.5 Checking the transition = seeing at which joint the state changes

Structure includes not only static arrangement, but also change of state.

Examples:
- the point at which a joint bends
- the point at which fixed and movable sections are divided
- the point at which the possibility of later joining changes depending on assembly order

That is, examine at which joint and under which condition the state changes.

### 3.6 Extracting patterns = finding recurring assembly methods

If one looks at multiple completed objects or multiple parts, repetition becomes visible.

Examples:
- repetition of the same type of joint connection
- repetition of similar dividing-line structures
- assembly methods that frequently appear in certain parts

That is, extract recurring structural patterns.

### 3.7 Checking exceptions = looking at part structures that differ from expectation

Not all completed objects are assembled in the same way.

Examples:
- cases where the exterior appears symmetrical but the internal structure is asymmetrical
- cases where there is no general dividing line and the form is treated as integrated
- cases where the assembly order has to be the reverse of what was expected

That is, check the exceptions.

### 3.8 Summarizing the structure = organizing the joining structure

Finally organize the following.

- what parts it is composed of
- where the boundaries are
- which joints are central
- where transitions occur
- what repetitions and exceptions there are

That is, organize the joining structure that made the completed object possible.

## 4. Correspondence Table of Generalized Structural Analysis Through the Plastic Model Analogy

If the plastic model analogy is translated into the language of generalized structural analysis, it is as follows.

- **surface observation** = looking at the outer appearance of the completed object  
- **structural analysis** = reverse inference of joining surfaces, dividing lines, and assembly order  
- **boundary recognition** = identifying up to where one part extends  
- **transition recognition** = seeing at which joint the state changes  
- **responsibility/judgment coordinate** = seeing which joining surface determines the overall form  

The core of this correspondence table is as follows.

1. Surface is a result.  
2. Structure is the mode of joining that made that result possible.  
3. A boundary is not a mere line, but a structural unit that divides what is joined to what.  
4. A transition is not a mere connection, but the point at which state and function change.  
5. The key joining surface that determines the overall form becomes an important judgment point in structural interpretation.

## 5. The General Limitation Shown by This Analogy

This analogy is useful for supporting understanding of generalized structural analysis, but does not itself explain all structural analysis.

This is because this document does not deal with the following.

- the proprietary formalization method by which structure is cut according to certain criteria
- how a specific structural unit is connected hierarchically to another structural unit
- how boundaries and transitions are fixed in document structure
- post-recognition judgment, changes in cost structure, and high-density observational systems after structural recognition

That is, this document is **an entry-level example for supporting understanding**, and is not a document that replaces the entire proprietary formalization of structural analysis.
